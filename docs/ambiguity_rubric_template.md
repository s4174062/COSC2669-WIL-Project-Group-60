# Ambiguity Rubric

**Status: DRAFT** 

## Our working definition of "ambiguous"

A question is ambiguous if it maps to more than one distinct policy
pathway in our source documents, where those pathways have materially
different processes, deadlines, or eligibility criteria — AND the
question as asked does not contain enough information to determine
which pathway applies.

A question is NOT ambiguous just because it's informally phrased, or
because it could theoretically be elaborated on — only when the missing
information would actually change which policy applies or what the
answer is.

## Criteria checklist

A question should be marked **ambiguous** if:
- [ ] It could reasonably map to two or more different policies/procedures in our documents with different rules (e.g. extension vs. special consideration; leave of absence vs. course withdrawal vs. deferred enrolment)
- [ ] The answer would materially differ depending on missing information (a different deadline, different approval authority, different required evidence)
- [ ] A reasonable RMIT staff member, hearing the question as asked, would also need to ask a follow-up before answering

A question should **NOT** be marked ambiguous just because:
- [ ] It's phrased casually or in a student's own words rather than policy language
- [ ] It's missing detail that doesn't change the answer (e.g. not stating which specific course, when only one policy applies regardless of course)
- [ ] It could theoretically be asked about a related topic, but as phrased has one clear best-fit answer

## Example questions — clearly ambiguous

1. **"I have an assessment in three days and need more time"** — maps to
   either an Extension (short-term, up to 7 calendar days, evidence must
   support the period) or Special Consideration (broader circumstances,
   normally applied for within 5 working days after the assessment, can
   result in equivalent assessment/deferral/late withdrawal) — genuinely
   different processes with different deadlines.
2. **"Can I take a break from my studies?"** — could mean Leave of
   Absence (defined period, max 50% of program duration, specific
   approval process) or Course Withdrawal (dropping a course, different
   deadlines and financial implications) — different documents, different
   answers.
3. **"I'm having financial difficulty, what can I do?"** — could relate
   to fee payment arrangements, Student Services and Amenities Fee
   queries, or (outside our current document set) financial hardship
   support — not resolvable from our documents without knowing which.

## Example questions — clearly NOT ambiguous

1. **"How long can an extension be granted for?"** — single policy
   pathway (Assessment and Assessment Flexibility Policy), single answer
   (up to 7 calendar days).
2. **"What's the minimum age to enrol at RMIT?"** — single fact, single
   source (Enrolment Procedure), no branching.
3. **"Can international students on a student visa take leave of absence
   for any reason?"** — specific enough (names the student cohort) that
   only one rule applies (compassionate/compelling grounds only).

## Borderline cases (discuss as a team)

1. **"Can I get an extension without documentation?"** — arguably not
   ambiguous (the policy answer is simply "no, evidence is required"),
   but could be read as ambiguous if the student actually meant "what's
   the minimum evidence needed" rather than a strict yes/no. Team
   decision: do we treat this as answerable directly, or as needing
   clarification on what "documentation" means to the student?
2. **"How do I defer my exam?"** — RMIT's documents don't use "defer" as
   a defined term for exams specifically; it appears as an outcome of
   special consideration ("a deferred assessment"). Is asking about
   deferral inherently pointing at special consideration, or ambiguous
   because the student might mean something else entirely?
3. **"I missed my special consideration deadline, what now?"** — the
   policy allows late applications with compelling/compassionate reasons
   and evidence. Does answering this need clarification on what the
   compelling reason is, or can the system give the general rule and let
   the student self-assess?
